## Important things I learned

I learned that companies often move to cloud computing because of cost savings, flexibility and scalability.  
Instead of buying and maintaining physical hardware, companies can use cloud services with a pay-per-use model.

I also learned the difference between **CapEx** and **OpEx**. CapEx means large investments like buying servers or equipment. OpEx means ongoing operating costs like subscriptions or service usage. Cloud computing can help organizations reduce large upfront costs.

The course explained the five main NIST characteristics of cloud computing:

- **Broad network access**
- **On-demand self-service**
- **Resource pooling**
- **Measured service**
- **Rapid elasticity**

An additional important concept is **multi-tenancy**, where multiple customers share the same cloud infrastructure, but their data must still be separated and protected.

I learned the difference between the three main cloud service models.  
In **IaaS**, the customer has the most control, for example over the operating system, storage and applications.  
In **PaaS**, the customer mainly controls the deployed applications and data, while the provider manages more of the platform.  
In **SaaS**, the customer has the least control and mainly uses the application provided by the cloud provider.

I also learned about the four deployment models.  
A **public cloud** is available to many customers through a provider.  
A **private cloud** is used by one organization.  
A **community cloud** is shared by organizations with similar requirements.  
A **hybrid cloud** combines two or more cloud models.

Virtualization was another important part. I learned that a **hypervisor** creates and runs virtual machines.  
A **Type 1 hypervisor** runs directly on the hardware and has a smaller attack surface.  
A **Type 2 hypervisor** runs on top of an operating system and can have more security risks because it depends on the host OS.

The course also explained the difference between **scalability** and **elasticity**.  
Scalability means adding resources to handle more work.  
Elasticity means resources can be added or removed based on demand.

I also learned that identity and access management is very important in cloud security.  
Important parts are identification, authentication, authorization, access management and de-provisioning.  
The course also explained multi-factor authentication, least privilege, privileged account management and federated identity with protocols like OAuth and SAML.

Another important topic was cloud design requirements. Before moving to the cloud, an organization needs to understand its assets, processes, business requirements and risks.  
A business impact analysis helps identify critical systems, maximum downtime, recovery goals and single points of failure.

I also learned that cloud customers are still responsible for their data, even when the data is stored or processed by a cloud provider.  
This means contracts, security policies, encryption, logging and audits are very important.

For protecting sensitive information, the course focused a lot on defense in depth, hardening, encryption, data loss prevention and key management.  
Encryption can protect data at rest, data in use and data in transit.  
Key management is especially important because if encryption keys are lost, the data may also be lost.

The last important topic was threat modeling.  
I learned about the **STRIDE** model:

- Spoofing
- Tampering
- Repudiation
- Information disclosure
- Denial of service
- Elevation of privilege

I also learned about **DREAD**, which is used to prioritize threats based on damage, reproducibility, exploitability, affected users and discoverability.
