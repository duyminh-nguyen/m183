## Important things I learned

I learned that the CCSP course is divided into six main security domains. These domains cover cloud architecture, cloud data security, platform and infrastructure security, application security, security operations, and legal risk and compliance.

A central topic was the **CIA Triad**. It describes the three main goals of information security:

- **Confidentiality** means that information should only be accessible to authorized users, systems or processes.
- **Integrity** means that information should not be changed, deleted or manipulated without authorization.
- **Availability** means that systems and information should be accessible when authorized users need them.

I also learned that the opposite of the CIA Triad is sometimes called **DAD**:

- **Disclosure** is the opposite of confidentiality.
- **Alteration** is the opposite of integrity.
- **Destruction** is the opposite of availability.

The course also explained that cybersecurity and information security are connected, but not exactly the same.  
Information security can include all formats of information, like paper documents, digital files or verbal information.  
Cybersecurity focuses more on digital systems, networks, programs and data.

Governance was another important topic. I learned that governance describes how an organization manages security, makes decisions and assigns responsibilities.  
Policies define what management expects, while procedures, standards, baselines and guidelines help implement these expectations in practice.

I also learned that security frameworks help organizations manage risk in a structured way. Examples are NIST, ISO 27001, CIS Controls, COBIT and PCI DSS.  
Frameworks help organizations choose security controls, reduce risk and make security more measurable.

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework was explained with five main functions:

- **Identify**
- **Protect**
- **Detect**
- **Respond**
- **Recover**

These functions help an organization understand its current cybersecurity situation, define a target state and improve security step by step.

In the compliance part, I learned that companies must follow legal and regulatory requirements depending on their location, business type and the data they process.  
Important terms were **incident**, **breach**, **data disclosure**, **due care** and **due diligence**.

I also started learning about cloud computing concepts.  
One reason companies move to the cloud is to reduce costs, especially capital expenditures like buying hardware and maintaining data centers. Cloud services often use an operational expenditure model where companies pay for what they use.

I learned that cloud computing has important benefits like agility, scalability, elasticity, mobility and virtualization.  
This means companies can access resources faster, scale based on demand and avoid managing all physical infrastructure themselves.

The course also explained the five NIST characteristics of cloud computing:

- **Broad network access**
- **On-demand self-service**
- **Resource pooling**
- **Measured service**
- **Rapid elasticity**

Another important term was **multi-tenancy**. This means multiple customers can share the same cloud infrastructure, but their data and resources must still be logically separated and protected from each other.
